# Channel playbook: @zackdfilms "health" Shorts (top 15 by views)

What the 15 most-viewed results for "health" on @zackdfilms have in common, measured on 2026-11-08 (76 M–256 M views each). Per-video reports are in `analysis/<nn>_<id>/REPORT.md` and numbers in `analysis/SUMMARY_TABLE.md`.
**Ground rules for our skill:** copy the format, not the content. Use our own characters, scripts, music, voice and channel name. Never use their name or logo (they hide "ZACK D FILMS" on props: a football field, a car door, a T-shirt, boxer shorts), footage or audio. Facts must be real and sourced.

---

## 1. The format in one line
A **~30 s vertical 3D render** of one body (or creature) process. It **opens mid-action at frame 0**, a calm male voice-over explains it **literally, one sentence per stage**, the camera **dives inside the body and back out**, and short **white captions** run the whole time. A very **quiet music bed** sits underneath, and it often **ends on its first frame so it loops**.

## 3. Script formula
Every script is 4–6 plain sentences. Each sentence is one visual beat lasting about 4–7 s. There's no intro, no "hey guys", no call to action, no outro.

**Opening lines that worked (pattern → example):**
- **"If you [do/suffer X], …"**: "If you get a splinter…", "If you touch a cactus…", "If you puncture a lung…", "If you cut a pilonidal cyst in half…", "If you lose one of your adult teeth…", "If you lost your finger, most people assume…" *(8 of 15, the most common opener)*
- **"When [everyday medical moment], …"**: "When you get stitches…", "When a doctor gives you a shot…", "When you swallow food…"
- **"If you somehow [absurd hypothetical]…"**: "…removed all of the blood vessels in your body and tied them together", "…took out one of your bones and molded it into a chunk"
- **Straight to the culprit:** "The parasite enters through the gills…"
- **Secret / myth:** "There is a secret about Skittles that the company doesn't want you to know about." / "Did you know the tongue is actually not the strongest muscle…?"
- **Third-person true story:** "He stuck a Lego piece up his nose and it got stuck…"

**Turn words that mark each beat** (each one usually lands on a transition):
"But sometimes…", "you might think… But…", "So instead…", "To stop it… Next…", "Now…", "And if you're lucky…", "And over a few weeks…", "So what is…? Well…"

**Story shapes (pick one):**
1. **Process:** the action → each biological stage → the end state (stitches, blown vein, bone graft).
2. **Problem → hidden cause → body fixes it:** splinter pushed out by pus pressure.
3. **Mistake → why it fails → "So instead" hack → satisfying result:** cactus spines and glue.
4. **"You might think…" → shown failing → "But…" look inside → same test passes:** bone cube against car (a mirrored test).
5. **Scale escalation:** body → football field → country → globe → "2 to 4 times over".
6. **First-aid steps:** emergency → X-ray shows the danger → numbered fix acted out by the person → rest pose.
7. **Myth-bust:** belief → "actually…" → answer highlighted → real-world proof.
8. **Hope twist:** scary loss → "most people assume…" → "But if…" conditions → step-by-step repair → perfect fix (severed finger).
9. **True story over time:** a kid's mistake → partial fix leaves something hidden (it glows so you can track it) → years pass (the character ages) → symptoms → sudden gross payoff that repeats the title's number (Lego in nose, 26 years).

**Tone:** calm, literal and factual, even for gross subjects. No hype words apart from the occasional emotional label ("this creepy thing"). The last sentence is the payoff fact or result, often ending on a number ("two to four times over", "nearly five standard cars", "200 pounds").

## 4. Visual style
- **Look:** clean, glossy, slightly stylised photoreal 3D. Smooth skin with some texture, wet and glossy organs, toy-like props and creatures.
- **Background:** a **seamless studio void**, usually **sky blue** (#5dacff–#b5e3f0). The colour can match the topic: **white** for dental/clinical, **deep purple** for the throat, **black** for anatomy breakdowns, **underwater gradient** for sea creatures, a blue grid wall. No sets or rooms; at most an implied floor with soft shadows.
- **People:** often **no face at all** (a calf, a hand, a forearm, a mouth). When there is a person, it's a young, generic, realistic adult in plain clothes, with **simple readable reactions** (startle, worry, astonishment). The newer videos use more of them.
- **Tools move by themselves:** needles, tweezers, scissors, glue bottles, implants and scalpels fly in, do the job and fly out, with no hands holding them.
- **Colour coding carries the explanation:** red = inflammation/arteries/danger, blue = veins/blood stopped, yellow = collagen/pus/fat/bone, green = nerves/airway, violet = food path, cyan = "pressure" highlight, a glowing yellow highlight = "this is the answer". Meaning is shown by **recolouring** an object at the moment its word is spoken.
- **Ways into the body without cutting away:**
  - the camera **pushes in until the surface fills the frame**, then continues "inside"
  - a **skin window** (an oval cut-away the camera flies through)
  - a **see-through material fade** (gum → bone, shirt → spine, whole body → grey X-ray)
  - a **cross-section block** (a skin cube showing layers)
  - a **mouth portal** (fly in through the lips and back out)
  - an **organ overlay** drawn on top of the clothed body
  - a **nose/body cut-away slice** returned to again and again (Lego)
- **Tracking highlights:** a hidden object glows (cyan) so viewers can follow it across years; the "answer" muscle glows yellow.
- **Showing time:** a fast pull-out, a whip, or a lighting time-lapse (sunset → night → day) plus a match-dissolve that ages the character.
- **Mixed footage (older videos, 2022–early 2024):** some top videos drop in real stock footage (a hospital corridor, a woman with a lollipop, a lab press) or a talking-head host. The newer 2024–25 videos are fully custom 3D, so our skill should be **fully 3D**. Where they used stock footage, we make a simple 3D scene instead.
- **Light:** bright, soft, high-key studio light from the upper left; warm skin against a cool background. Light vignette in the close-ups. Brightness 0.65–0.89, saturation 0.3–0.7, contrast low (0.14–0.28).

## 5. Camera
- One CG camera with **smooth eased moves the whole time**. No handheld shake and no rack focus.
- **Signature move: push-in → hold → deeper push-in → glide → fast pull-out.** The fast pull-out often stands for "time passes" (video 01: divergence -14.7, the fastest move in the video).
- Shot sizes go medium → close → **extreme close-up/macro** → back out. Common angles: low "landscape" angles inside the body (vessels look monumental) and **ground-level skin macro** (hairs like trees).
- Depth of field: shallow in the close-ups, deep in the wides.
- For scale stories: crane-ups to a top-down view, fast aerial fly-overs, and a pull-out to space.

## 6. Editing
- **Few or no hard cuts.** Several top videos are a **single continuous shot** (01, 04).
- Scene changes are made by:
  - camera moves through a surface (a window, the mouth, a push into the skin)
  - **3D cube/door flip:** the frame turns on a vertical axis with black behind it. The signature editor transition, used on "But…/So instead…" turns.
  - **zoom-blur / radial blur**
  - **whip-pan** with motion blur
  - **material fade or recolour**
- **Loop:** about half the videos end on (nearly) the same framing as frame 0, and the last sentence runs straight into the first. The others **bookend**: they end on the fixed or finished version of the opening shot.
- **Rhythm:** a new beat every ~4–7 s, tied to sentences. The voice starts at frame 0 and runs to within ~0.5 s of the end.

## 7. Captions (on every video since 2023)
- **White, bold, rounded geometric sans-serif, sentence case, with a soft dark drop shadow.** No box and no outline stroke.
- **1–3 words per card**, centred horizontally at **~78 % of frame height** (lower third).
- Cards change on spoken phrase boundaries (~0.75 s each). Numbers are written as digits ("2 to 4", "200lbs").
- No title card, no labels (one exception: the flavour labels in 11), no progress bar. Rare extra: a small white **line icon** (09).

## 8. Audio
- **Voice:** one adult male narrator, neutral American English, calm and clear, mid pitch. Even delivery with no shouting, at ~2.7 words/s.
- **Music:** a soft, light, mid-tempo bed (~95–115 BPM estimated), mixed **~18 dB under the voice**, almost background texture. It can go louder and darker for creepy topics (12: 10 dB under, dark bass).
- **SFX:** sparse, on actions only: a snip, a recoil whoosh, a pop, a snap, a whoosh on the fast pull-out or zoom. Typically 1–6 per video.
- No silence apart from the last ~0.3 s.

## 9. Topic picks that worked (by views)

## 10. Build checklist for our version
1. Topic = one body/nature process with a visible before → after. Gather 2–3 sources.
2. Script: 4–6 sentences, ~75–90 words (aim for 2.7 w/s at 28–34 s), opening with "If you…/When you…/If you somehow…", with a turn word on each beat and the payoff fact last.
3. Storyboard one shot per sentence. Plan the way into the body (push-in, window, see-through, cross-section, portal). Plan a recolour for every key word. Decide whether it loops or bookends.
4. Our own 3D world: a seamless void (sky blue by default, matched to topic), glossy stylised anatomy, self-moving tools, our own generic character (never their logo or people).
5. Camera: eased push-ins and pull-outs, a fast pull-out for time skips, 0–2 cuts, cube flips/zoom-blurs only on turn words.
6. Captions: white bold rounded with a soft shadow, 1–3 words, at 78 % height, digits for numbers.
7. Audio: calm male voice-over from frame 0 to the end; original soft music ~18 dB under; 1–6 SFX on actions.
8. Render 1080×1920 (or 2160×3840) at 30 fps, ~30 s. Check the first and last frames match if it loops.
