TITLE
What A Hot Chili Pepper Does To Your Body

ALTERNATIVE TITLES
Why Spicy Food Feels Like Fire
Why Water Won't Stop The Burn (But Milk Does)
Your Mouth Isn't Actually Burning

DESCRIPTION
If you bite into a hot chili pepper, a chemical called capsaicin grabs the heat sensors on your tongue (the TRPV1 receptor). These sensors normally only fire for real, painful heat, so they send a "fire alarm" to your brain. Your brain thinks your mouth is on fire, so you start sweating to cool down, your face turns red and your eyes water. But water barely helps, because capsaicin doesn't dissolve in water. Milk works much better, because its proteins help wash the capsaicin away. And the funny part? Your mouth never actually got any hotter.

How strongly you react depends on the pepper and on the person.
