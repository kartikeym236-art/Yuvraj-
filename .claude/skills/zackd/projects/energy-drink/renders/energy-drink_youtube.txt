DESCRIPTION
If you drink an energy drink, about seven teaspoons of sugar rush into your blood, and your pancreas pumps out insulin to soak it up. Then the caffeine reaches your brain and blocks adenosine, the signal that makes you feel tired. It also triggers adrenaline, so your heart can beat faster and your blood pressure rises. But when the sugar drops and the caffeine wears off, the tiredness it was hiding can hit you at once, and five hours later about half the caffeine is still in your body.

Figures are for a typical 250 ml can (about 27 g sugar, about 80 mg caffeine). Effects vary from person to person.
