Imagine a donkey standing exactly halfway between two identical bales of hay: same size, same distance, same smell, completely equal in every way. The donkey is hungry, but because both options are perfectly identical, it has no rational reason to choose one over the other. So it stands there, unable to decide, and eventually it starves to death.
If two options are truly equal, what makes you choose one over the other? Pure reason cannot decide between identical options, so either the choice is made by something other than reason, or the options were never truly equal to begin with.
It shows up in real life: choosing between two jobs that seem equally good, two apartments, two people you have feelings for. The more rationally you approach an identical choice, the more paralyzed you become.
Philosophers use this paradox to argue about free will. If a perfectly rational agent cannot choose between equal options without some external nudge, what exactly is making the choice in humans? Is it reason? Is it desire? Is it something beneath both that we never fully control?
