1. Meaning and Definitions

Kid storyA class wants to pick a picnic spot. The teacher doesn't decide alone. Every child votes, the spot with the most votes wins, and children who wanted another spot still get to speak. That is democracy: everyone gets a say, the majority decides, and the minority is respected.

Democracy is a form of government in which power belongs to the people, who rule either directly or through representatives they choose in free and fair elections.

  • Word origin: Greek demos = people + kratos = power or rule. Democracy = rule of the people.
  • Abraham Lincoln (Gettysburg Address, 1863): "Government of the people, by the people, for the people." The most quoted definition.
  • J. R. Seeley: "Democracy is a government in which everyone has a share."
  • Lord Bryce: rule of the people expressing their sovereign will through the vote.
  • Dr B. R. Ambedkar: a form and method of government by which revolutionary changes in social and economic life are brought about without bloodshed.

Democracy is not only a form of government. It is also a way of life (equality, respect, discussion), a social system (no caste or class privilege) and an economic idea (fair chances for all).

Memory trick"OBF": Of, By, For the people. Lincoln's three little words hold the whole idea.

3. Types of Democracy

A. By how people rule

TypeMeaningExample
Direct democracyPeople themselves make laws and decisionsAncient Athens; Swiss cantons (Landsgemeinde); Gram Sabha in Indian villages
Indirect / representative democracyPeople elect representatives who make laws for themIndia, UK, USA

Tools of direct democracy (used inside representative systems): Referendum (people vote on a law), Initiative (people propose a law), Recall (people remove a representative before the term ends), Plebiscite (people vote on an important public question, such as the status of a territory).

B. By relation of executive and legislature

PointParliamentary (India, UK)Presidential (USA)
Real headPrime MinisterPresident
Head of statePresident / Monarch (nominal)President (same person)
Executive comes fromThe legislature (ministers are MPs)Separately elected; ministers are not legislators
Responsible toLegislature; can be removed by a no-confidence motionFixed term; removed only by impeachment
StrengthResponsible, flexibleStable, clear separation of powers

C. Other forms you should know

  • Constitutional monarchy: a king or queen is head of state but elected leaders rule (UK, Japan).
  • Republic: head of state is elected, not hereditary (India, USA).
  • Liberal democracy: elections plus strong protection of individual rights.
  • Social democracy: democracy plus a welfare state that reduces inequality (Nordic countries).
  • Participatory / deliberative democracy: citizens take part through discussion, not only voting.
  • E-democracy: using digital tools for participation (online petitions, e-governance).

4. Features and Principles of Democracy

Remember "FREE PEOPLE":

FeatureMeaning in one line
FFree and fair electionsRegular elections where people can really choose and the ruling party can lose
RRule of lawEveryone, including leaders, is equal before the law
EEqualityOne person, one vote, one value; no discrimination
EEvery adult votes (universal adult franchise)All citizens above a set age (18 in India) can vote
PPopular sovereigntyFinal power belongs to the people
EExpression is freeFreedom of speech, press and association
OOpposition and many partiesMultiparty system and a respected opposition
PProtection of rights and minoritiesMajority rules, but minority rights are protected
LLimited government and separation of powersConstitution limits power; legislature, executive and judiciary check each other
EEnforceable accountabilityGovernment answers to people, courts, media; independent judiciary
Exam pointDemocracy is not just majority rule. A government elected by majority that crushes minorities, courts or the press is called an illiberal democracy or "tyranny of the majority" (phrase used by Alexis de Tocqueville and J. S. Mill).

5. Pillars of Democracy

Kid storyA house stands on four strong pillars. If one cracks, the roof wobbles. Democracy also stands on four pillars.
PillarJobIn India
1. LegislatureMakes laws, controls money, questions the governmentParliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha + President); State Legislatures
2. ExecutiveImplements laws and runs the governmentPresident, PM and Council of Ministers, civil services
3. JudiciaryInterprets laws, protects rights, settles disputesSupreme Court, High Courts, lower courts
4. Media (Fourth Estate)Informs people and watches the powerfulNewspapers, TV, digital media

Separation of powers was explained by Montesquieu in The Spirit of the Laws (1748). India follows a flexible separation with checks and balances, e.g. courts can strike down unconstitutional laws (judicial review).

Memory trick"LEJ-M": Laws, Enforcement, Justice, Media.

6. Democracy in India

India is the world's largest democracy: a parliamentary, federal, constitutional republic. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. Dr B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee.

The Preamble

Preamble keywordsIndia is a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic, securing to all citizens Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty (of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship), Equality (of status and opportunity) and Fraternity (dignity of the individual, unity and integrity of the Nation).
"Socialist", "Secular" and "integrity" were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Memory trick"SSSDR" for the type of State, and "JLEF" (Just Like Every Family) for the four ideals.

Key constitutional provisions

ProvisionWhat it does
Part III, Articles 12–35Fundamental Rights: equality (14–18), freedom (19–22), against exploitation (23–24), religion (25–28), cultural and educational (29–30), constitutional remedies (32)

Three levels of democracy in India

LevelLawmaking bodyHead of government
Union (Centre)Parliament: Lok Sabha (543 elected seats) and Rajya Sabha (up to 250 members)Prime Minister
StateVidhan Sabha (and Vidhan Parishad in some states)Chief Minister
LocalGram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad; Municipalities and CorporationsSarpanch, Mayor, etc.

The Gram Sabha (all adult voters of a village) is the closest thing to direct democracy in India.

Testing times

The Emergency (1975–77) suspended many rights and postponed elections. In 1977 voters defeated the ruling party, showing that Indian voters defend democracy. The 44th Amendment (1978) then added safeguards against misuse of emergency powers.

Right to Information Act, 2005 lets any citizen ask the government for information, increasing transparency and accountability.

7. Elections in India

  • Election Commission of India was set up on 25 January 1950; this date is celebrated as National Voters' Day (since 2011).
  • India follows First-Past-The-Post (FPTP): the candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins, even without 50%.
  • The President and Rajya Sabha members are elected indirectly; the President's election uses proportional representation by single transferable vote.
  • Model Code of Conduct: rules for parties and candidates from the announcement of elections.
  • EVMs (electronic voting machines) and VVPAT (a paper slip that lets voters verify their vote).
  • NOTA ("None of the Above") option added in 2013 after a Supreme Court judgment.
  • Reserved constituencies for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes ensure their representation.
  • Indelible ink on the finger prevents double voting.
Memory trick"EC on 25-1-50": Election Commission born a day before the Republic (26-1-50).

9. Merits and Demerits

Merits

  1. Protects rights and dignity of people
  2. Government is accountable and can be changed peacefully
  3. Based on equality: one person, one vote
  4. Better decisions through discussion
  5. Resolves conflicts peacefully in diverse societies
  6. Gives political education to citizens
  7. Allows people to correct mistakes at the next election
  8. Encourages patriotism and participation

Demerits (criticisms)

  1. Slow decisions due to debate
  2. Leaders change, so policies may be unstable
  3. Rule of numbers, not wisdom (Plato's criticism)
  4. Costly elections; money power
  5. Can lead to corruption and vote-bank politics
  6. Risk of majority tyranny
  7. Uninformed voters can be misled
  8. Short-term thinking to win the next election
Exam conclusion lineDespite its demerits, democracy is better than other forms because it treats every citizen as equal and allows mistakes to be corrected peacefully.

10. Challenges to Democracy

Remember "CRIME FILM":

ChallengeMeaning
CCasteism and communalismVoting and politics based on caste or religion instead of issues
RRegionalismNarrow regional loyalty against national unity
IIlliteracy and lack of awarenessVoters cannot judge policies well
MMoney and muscle powerRich or violent candidates dominate elections
EEconomic inequality and povertyVotes bought for small gains; poor feel left out
FFake news and misinformationSocial media spreads lies that mislead voters
IInner-party democracy missingParties run by one family or leader
LLow participationLow voter turnout, especially among the youth and in cities
MMisuse of power and corruptionLeaders weaken courts, media or opposition ("democratic backsliding")

How to strengthen democracy

  • Electoral reforms: state funding of elections, transparency in party funding, keeping criminals out.
  • Education and awareness: voter education (e.g. the Election Commission's SVEEP programme), media literacy.
  • Strong institutions: independent judiciary, Election Commission, free press, RTI.
  • Decentralisation: empower Panchayats and Municipalities.
  • Active citizens: vote, question leaders, respect others' rights, perform fundamental duties.

11. Democracy and Education

Kid storyA child who is never allowed to speak in class grows into an adult who never speaks up. A child who votes for the class monitor, makes class rules and listens to others learns democracy by living it.
  • John Dewey (Democracy and Education, 1916): the school is a miniature society; children learn democracy by practising it.
  • Education creates informed, thinking voters. Democracy, in turn, gives education to all (RTE Act 2009, Article 21A).
  • NEP 2020 asks schools to develop constitutional values: democracy, justice, liberty, equality, fraternity, secularism and respect for diversity.
  • Kothari Commission (1964–66) linked education with national development and democracy.

Methods that build democratic values: discussion, debate, mock parliament, role play, project work, cooperative learning, celebrating Constitution Day.
