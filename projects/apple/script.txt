There is a mathematical proof that if you seal an apple inside a box and wait for an extremely long time, that apple can reform. Let's talk about it.
Imagine a perfectly insulated, airtight box with an apple inside, where all the air has been pumped out. Now, what does the Second Law of Thermodynamics say will happen? The apple will slowly decompose, and its atoms will spread throughout the entire box. But look closely: every single atom of the apple is still inside that box. Only their arrangement has changed, and they are constantly in motion.
Now, how can this apple reform? Behind this lies a mathematical theorem. In {1890|eighteen ninety}, Henri {Poincaré|pwan karay} proved that if a closed system is confined within a finite space, it will eventually return arbitrarily close to its initial state— and this can happen repeatedly.
However, there is another crazy twist. Returning doesn't mean what you might think. No one is actively reassembling the apple; this occurs purely by chance.
Think of it like this: imagine a computer randomly guessing a password. If it continues trying endlessly, theoretically one day it will guess the correct password. The apple's "password" is more than ten trillion trillion characters long. That means every single atom of the apple must be in the exact right place and moving in the exact right direction.
"Arbitrarily close" means the apple won't be perfectly identical, but it will be close enough that you wouldn't even notice the difference.
So, how long would you have to wait for this? This {timescale|time scale} grows exponentially with the number of atoms. In {2024|twenty twenty four}, Dominik Levesque and Nicolas {Sourlas|soor lah} simulated a gas and estimated that even with just a few hundred particles, such a crude return could take longer than the age of the universe. If we're talking about an entire apple, the number of years required to wait would contain hundreds of billions of trillions of digits.
Yet, the moment the apple reappears, its atoms will still be in constant motion, and it would immediately fall apart again.
So physics says the apple will return, but the wait for it to reform is so long that you won't be around to observe it.
