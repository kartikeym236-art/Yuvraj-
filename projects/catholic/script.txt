Catholicism is one of the oldest and largest branches of Christianity.
Catholics believe in one God who exists as three persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.
This is called the Holy Trinity.
And Jesus Christ? Catholics believe he is the Son of God, who died for humanity's sins and rose from the dead.
Now, here's where things get interesting.
Catholics have a special leader called the Pope.
He lives in Vatican City, a tiny independent country surrounded by Rome, Italy.
The Pope is considered the successor of Saint Peter, whom Catholics believe Jesus appointed to lead his followers.
Think of him as the spiritual leader of the worldwide Catholic Church.
Catholics follow the Bible, but they also follow Sacred Tradition and the teachings of the Church.
They deeply respect Mary, the mother of Jesus.
And they honour saints, people believed to have lived exceptionally holy lives.
Catholics may ask saints to pray for them, just as you might ask a friend to pray for you.
But worship? That's reserved for God alone.
Catholicism also has seven special religious practices called sacraments.
These include baptism, confession, marriage, and the Eucharist.
And remember that bread from the beginning?
During the Eucharist, Catholics believe the bread and wine truly become the body and blood of Christ, even though their outward appearance remains unchanged.
This belief is called transubstantiation.
So, Catholicism isn't just about going to church on Sunday.
