In a small pond, there lived a hundred fish. And there was a rule: do not go past the rocks. Whoever goes, never returns.
A young fish asked an elder fish, "What lies beyond the rocks?"
The elder replied, "Nothing, only darkness."
So, the young fish stayed back. Days passed, years passed— the same water, the same rocks, the same fear.
Then, one summer, the pond began to dry up. With no other choice left, she swam past the rocks. And what did she find there? A river.
The river flowed into the sea, and in the sea, there were thousands of fish. Every single one had left a small pond at some point.
She asked an elder fish there, "Did anyone ever go back?"
The elder laughed, "Why would anyone go back? Water dries up there; here, it never does. That is why no one ever returned— not because they died, but because they found something better."
Meanwhile, back at the pond, the same old story continued to be told, just to keep everyone else living in fear.
You aren't trapped; you have simply been told that you are. Those are two very different things.
