Even the man set to become the world's first trillionaire bows to the sheikhs of Dubai. And here's the surprising part. You'll probably never see the names of Dubai's ruling sheikhs on any billionaire list. Elon Musk knows he isn't really the richest or most powerful man alive.
Here's why. Musk's trillion dollar fortune is tied up in shares of his companies. It's not sitting in his bank account. Even if he wanted to, he couldn't just sell those shares and cash out a trillion dollars. A selloff that big could rattle the entire {US|u s} stock market, and a {US|u s} market crash can drag down the global economy.
Second, the moment he starts selling, the supply of shares floods the market, but demand doesn't keep up. The price would collapse, and his trillion dollar companies would sell for pennies on the dollar. Musk isn't going to shoot himself in the foot like that.
Dubai's royal families, on the other hand, are believed to control enormous liquid wealth. Cash, bank balances, gold, real estate, supercars. And nobody outside really knows where all of it comes from.
So while Musk has net worth on paper, the sheikhs have liquidity, influence, and control of the system. And even the richest man on paper has to play by the rules of that system. That's why he bows.
