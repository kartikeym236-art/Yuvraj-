Is the world really going to end tomorrow, on November {13th|thirteenth}? Why is everyone saying this? Because this date wasn't predicted by a mystic, but was calculated {66|sixty six} years ago by three scientists and published in one of the world's top science magazines. They wrote that future generations would not die of hunger, but would be squeezed to death by overcrowding.
The alarming part is that their formula remained roughly accurate for the next {30|thirty} years. But how did a formula predict the exact end of the world?
Consider this: it took humanity hundreds of thousands of years to reach the first {1 billion|one billion} people. The next {1 billion|one billion} took just {123|one hundred twenty three} years, and the one after that took only {33|thirty three} years. As the population grew, so did new ideas— leading to better medicine, more food, lower death rates, and consequently, even more people.
When the scientists projected this accelerating pace forward, the interval between population leaps shrank until it would eventually hit zero. In other words, the population would become so immense that there wouldn't even be room left to stand on Earth. That calculated date turned out to be Friday, November {13, 2026|thirteenth twenty twenty six}.
So, how did we survive? Those same scientists had also proposed the solution: the world needed to cut its birth rates in half. And that is precisely what happened. Today, the average number of children per woman globally is about half of what it was in {1960|nineteen sixty}, and in India, it has dropped from around six to two.
This means their formula wasn't wrong; humanity simply changed course. Therefore, the world will not end tomorrow— it will just be another ordinary Friday.
