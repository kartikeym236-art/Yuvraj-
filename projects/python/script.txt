A woman let her pet python sleep beside her in bed every single night. It was {10 ft|ten feet} long, and she loved it like a child. Then, one week, it stopped eating. She offered rabbits, chicken, its favorite mice; it turned away from all of them. But every night, it did something strange. It slid under her blanket and stretched its whole body out perfectly straight right next to hers, head at her head, tail at her feet. She thought it was sick, maybe lonely, so she held it closer. Night after night, it grew thinner and pressed tighter against her.
Finally, she took it to a vet and told him everything: the fasting, the stretching, the cuddling. The vet went quiet. Then he said, "Your snake isn't sick, and it isn't cuddling you. It's emptying its stomach to make room, and every night it lies beside you to measure if you'll fit."
That's the scariest part. It never looked like an attack; it looked like love. Psychologists call this grooming. Before someone harms you, they get close, closer than anyone. They make it feel special while they quietly measure how much you'll allow.
The partner who cuts you off from friends because they love you. The boss who praises you right before he asks for more. The stranger online who always knows exactly what to say. Real love doesn't need to measure you.
So ask yourself: who's been lying a little too close?
