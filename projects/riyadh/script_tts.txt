Saudi Arabia's capital, Riyadh, has been hit again. Witnesses say the target was the same tower struck five days ago. And this time, the usual emergency alert never reached people's phones. But what the Houthis are demanding to end this war will shock you.
This is the eighty-floor PIF Tower. It's where Saudi Arabia runs its roughly 900 billion dollar government investment fund. Last week, a Houthi missile reportedly wrecked several of its floors. The Saudi-led coalition confirmed today's attack but didn't name the building. No injuries have been reported so far, and the full details still aren't public.
But this isn't about one building. On October 10th, Riyadh's airport was hit, reportedly killing 12 people and injuring 309. The next day, it was hit again. This month, an Aramco tower was struck too. And Saudi Arabia had to cancel its biggest investor event, known as Davos in the Desert.
Look at the pattern. First the airport. Then the oil giant. Now the building that manages the country's money. My theory? The Houthis aren't just attacking buildings. They're attacking investor confidence. The message is simple: your money isn't safe here.
And now the numbers. According to the Wall Street Journal, the Houthis are demanding 51 billion dollars to stop. They also want Saudi Arabia to recognize their government in Yemen. Saudi Arabia hasn't said yes or no, and talks are still ongoing.
