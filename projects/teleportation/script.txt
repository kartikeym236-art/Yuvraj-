To teleport, you would first have to be destroyed. And whoever arrived would have your face, your voice, and all your memories. But it might not be you. And it wouldn't be a machine failure; it's imposed by the laws of physics.
Teleportation already exists, but only with particles. In {2017|twenty seventeen}, it was achieved with particles of light between the ground and a satellite at a distance of up to {1,400|one thousand four hundred} kilometers. What travels is not the particle, but its information. On the other side, a second particle receives it and becomes an exact replica of the first. But physics forbids copying a particle while preserving the original. Therefore, by reading it, its original state is destroyed.
Now let's apply that rule to your body, which contains about {7,000|seven thousand} quadrillion atoms. A machine would have to read all of them, and that scanning would destroy you on Earth. Your information would travel to Mars at the speed of light. And there, an identical person would be assembled, atom by atom. They would open their eyes, remember stepping into the booth, and say, "I've arrived."
