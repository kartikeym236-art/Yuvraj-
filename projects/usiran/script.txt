Why are America and Iran always fighting? It all started with oil.
Iran sits on some of the largest oil reserves on Earth. But in the early {1950s|nineteen fifties}, a British company controlled it, and Iran got only about {16|sixteen} percent of the profits.
In {1951|nineteen fifty one}, Iran's democratically elected prime minister, Mohammad Mosaddegh, nationalized the oil. Britain was furious. So in {1953|nineteen fifty three}, the {CIA|c i a} and British intelligence ran Operation Ajax and overthrew him.
They put the Shah, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, back in full power. He ruled for {26|twenty six} years, and American companies got a {40|forty} percent share of Iran's oil. But his secret police tortured opponents and crushed dissent.
In {1979|nineteen seventy nine}, Iranians rose up. The Shah fled, and Ayatollah Khomeini took power. When the Shah was let into the {US|u s}, Iranian students stormed the American embassy and held {52|fifty two} Americans hostage for {444|four hundred forty four} days. They feared another coup.
Then in {1980|nineteen eighty}, Saddam Hussein's Iraq invaded Iran, and over time the {US|u s} backed Iraq with intelligence. Hundreds of thousands died.
Iran answered by funding groups like Hezbollah and Hamas, and building a nuclear program. America answered with sanctions.
In {2015|twenty fifteen}, Obama's deal lifted sanctions in exchange for strict nuclear limits. Trump tore it up in {2018|twenty eighteen}, calling it the worst deal ever. In his second term, talks failed, sanctions returned, and the {US|u s} and Israel struck Iran directly.
Seventy years of oil, coups and revenge. And it's still not over.
