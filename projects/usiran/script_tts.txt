Why are America and Iran always fighting? It all started with oil.
Iran sits on some of the largest oil reserves on Earth. But in the early 1950s, a British company controlled it, and Iran got only about 16 percent of the profits.
In 1951, Iran's democratically elected prime minister, Mohammad Mosaddegh, nationalized the oil. Britain was furious. So in 1953, the CIA and British intelligence ran Operation Ajax and overthrew him.
They put the Shah, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, back in full power. He ruled for 26 years, and American companies got a 40 percent share of Iran's oil. But his secret police tortured opponents and crushed dissent.
In 1979, Iranians rose up. The Shah fled, and Ayatollah Khomeini took power. When the Shah was let into the US, Iranian students stormed the American embassy and held 52 Americans hostage for 444 days. They feared another coup.
Then in 1980, Saddam Hussein's Iraq invaded Iran, and over time the US backed Iraq with intelligence. Hundreds of thousands died.
Iran answered by funding groups like Hezbollah and Hamas, and building a nuclear program. America answered with sanctions.
In 2015, Obama's deal lifted sanctions in exchange for strict nuclear limits. Trump tore it up in 2018, calling it the worst deal ever. In his second term, talks failed, sanctions returned, and the US and Israel struck Iran directly.
Seventy years of oil, coups and revenge. And it's still not over.
