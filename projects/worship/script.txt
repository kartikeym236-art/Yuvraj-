Let's concede for a moment that God created this universe. He made me, He made this entire Earth— whatever exists, God created it all. But just for this reason, should we worship God?
Let's understand this through an example. Your parents brought you into this world; in short, they made you. You can be grateful to them, you can respect them. But what if those very parents beat you, starved you, watched other children suffer in agony right in front of you and did nothing, and told you, "I gave you life, therefore worship me"? Would that be right?
The obvious answer is no. This teaches us one clear thing: worship isn't granted merely for creating something; worship is earned through goodness.
Now, look at God. God created this world, yet in this very world, children die of cancer, some die the moment they are born, others starve to death, people get raped, and terrible atrocities occur. God watches all of this and has the power to stop it, yet He doesn't. So, is worshipping God merely because He created this universe really enough?
Some people argue, "He is {all-powerful|all powerful} and {all-knowing|all knowing}, therefore you must worship Him." That is not worship; that is merely fear. True worship happens when the one being revered is genuinely good— otherwise, some people even revered Hitler.
This brings to mind a quote by the philosopher John Stuart Mill. He said that if there is a God who will punish me simply because I refuse to call Him good when His actions are not good, then I am prepared to accept that punishment, but I will not call Him good.
And another thing: why would a creator who built this entire universe and lacks nothing desire that people bow down before Him and worship Him?
So the question isn't whether God exists or not. The real question is: even if He does exist, is He truly worthy of worship?
