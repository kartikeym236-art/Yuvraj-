Like Jensen, Mark Zuckerberg can never be ousted from his company. Because in 2006, Yahoo made an offer to buy Facebook for 1 billion dollars. At that time, all the board investors pressured Zuckerberg to sell Facebook. But Zuckerberg understood right then that if he didn't take voting control into his own hands, the same thing would happen to him as happened to Steve Jobs.
So, he created a system back then, and divided all the shares into two classes: Class A and Class B. In Class A, there is the general public and big investors, where one share is equal to one vote. Second is Class B. In this, nearly 99.8% of the shares belong to Mark Zuckerberg alone, and in this class, one share is worth 10 times more. Meaning one share equals 10 votes.
Now, Zuckerberg owns only a 13.4% stake in Meta, but because of the 10 votes per share structure, he holds a total of 61% of the voting rights. To make any major decision in the company, more than 50% of the votes are required, whereas Zuckerberg himself holds 61%. This means that even if all the investors come together, they cannot oust Zuckerberg from Meta. On the contrary, Zuckerberg himself decides who stays on the board and who doesn't.
