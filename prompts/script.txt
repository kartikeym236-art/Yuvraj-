In 1993, Jensen Huang co-founded Nvidia. Before that, the computer world was just playing around with CPUs, but when Nvidia introduced the world's first GPU in 1999, it created a massive stir in the tech industry. And this is where the real drama begins.
Back in 2008, a large Chinese gaming company called 7Road Holdings was established. They also started using Nvidia's GPUs. However, an investment in this firm was held by Shanghai Hexi Management. And who was its Executive Director? Alice Huang. In fact, back then, Alice was not even on the US government's radar.
Gradually, Nvidia moved beyond just GPUs and started developing AI superchips. Before long, Nvidia became the driving engine of the global AI revolution. That’s when the US government started getting fidgety. Initially, the US government informed Nvidia that selling its chips to any Chinese entity would require strict regulatory permission. To navigate around this, Nvidia found a loophole in 2022 and engineered two watered-down, export-grade chip models. But the US government wasn't pleased with that either. In 2023, they imposed a blanket ban on Nvidia, declaring that even these watered-down chips could no longer be sold to China.
Here is the twist: a few months earlier, in March, that same Chinese gaming company—7Road Holdings—had rebranded itself as Megaspeed International, a business focused on procuring and leasing AI chips. By November, Megaspeed came under complete Singaporean ownership, and just four days later, it became an official Nvidia Cloud Partner. The Managing Director of this company? Alice Huang.
Now Alice was directly on the radar of the US Department of Commerce. Between 2024 and 2026, when Megaspeed International's Indonesian sister company, Speed Matrix, purchased $4.8 billion worth of Nvidia superchips, the Commerce Department paused and wondered what this overnight company was really doing. The US Commerce Department raided Speed Matrix's Indonesian data center. During the raid, they uncovered massive quantities of Nvidia GPUs lying uninstalled inside their original shipping crates.
Given the scale of the matter, US intelligence agencies began investigating Alice aggressively. That inquiry revealed that Alice was in an intimate personal relationship with Nvidia founder Jensen Huang. Suddenly, in May 2025, a video of Jensen and Alice in Taipei went viral. Following this, in August 2025, Singaporean authorities barred Alice Huang from leaving the country until the investigation against her concluded.
Naturally, word reached Nvidia. In response, Nvidia counsel Teeter went straight to placing a direct phone call to the US Under Secretary of Commerce. Teeter then emailed Commerce Department field investigators gathering ground-level intelligence, stating that the matter had already been addressed with their superiors. Stunned by this pushback, the field agents escalated the case, bringing it straight to the Department of Justice.
In October, The New York Times published an investigative report laying out the full secret details to the public. To protect his position, Jensen Huang sent an internal email to his employees on Diwali.
