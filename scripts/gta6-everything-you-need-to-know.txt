First, the basics.

GTA Six takes place in the state of Leonida, Rockstar's version of Florida. And the heart of it is a place fans know very well. Vice City. Back for the first time since 2002, but this time, it's modern. Skyscrapers. Influencers. Neon nightlife. And a whole lot of chaos.

You play as two main characters. Lucia Caminos, who has just got out of prison on probation. And Jason Duval, a guy who wants an easy life, but keeps getting pulled into crime. Together, they're a Bonnie and Clyde style couple, trying to survive in one of the craziest places on Earth.

Now, let's talk about the cars.

From what fans have spotted, GTA Six could have some of the most realistic vehicles in the series. Quick reminder though, Rockstar never uses real car brands. Every car gets its own made-up name. But many of them are clearly inspired by real-world cars.

Here are some you might spot.

A car that looks just like the legendary Nissan Skyline GT-R R34. A dream car for every street racing fan.

One inspired by the Toyota GR86. Small, light, and perfect for drifting.

A classic American muscle car that looks like the Chevrolet Chevelle, with those racing stripes.

A mid-engine supercar that looks a lot like the Chevrolet Corvette C8.

A big, powerful luxury coupe that resembles the Mercedes-Benz CL65 AMG.

A rugged SUV inspired by the Land Rover Discovery.

A sporty little coupe that looks like the BMW M2.

A retro convertible inspired by the classic BMW 3 Series E30 Cabriolet. Pure eighties vibes, perfect for Vice City.

And of course, a giant pickup truck that looks just like the Ford F-150. Because it wouldn't be Florida without a big truck.

Next, the animals.

This is where things get wild. Literally. According to reports, GTA Six could feature over one hundred and seventy different animal species. That would be by far the most ever in a GTA game.

In the swamps and wetlands, you could find alligators, snakes, and iguanas. Exactly what you'd expect in Florida.

In the forests and wild areas, there could be Florida panthers, deer, bobcats, foxes, raccoons, wild boars, and even beavers.

Along the water, keep an eye out for flamingos, cranes, herons, ducks, and pelicans. And out in the ocean, dolphins.

And in the neighborhoods, pets. Cats, and dogs like Chihuahuas, Dobermans, German Shepherds, and Rottweilers.

Rockstar showed off a lot of wildlife in the trailers. The alligator in the convenience store from the first trailer is already famous. So expect Leonida to feel incredibly alive.

Now, what can you actually do in Leonida? A lot.

Based on reports and what fans expect from the trailers, the list of activities could be huge.

You could go hunting in the wilderness. Base jumping off the mountains. Scuba diving under the sea. Kayaking along the coast.

You could hit the basketball court. Chill by the pool. Play mini golf. Go fishing off the pier.

There could be a gym to work out. A shooting range to practice your aim. MMA-style fighting. And even wrestling.

At night, you could head to the nightclubs. During the day, go shopping. And when you just want to relax, watch TV at home.

And if you love speed, there's street racing through the city, and off-road racing through the wilderness.

And then there's social media. In the trailers, social media is everywhere. People filming crimes, posting videos, going viral. It looks like it'll be a big part of how the world reacts to everything you do.

Speaking of social media, let's talk about the phone.

Every GTA game has a phone. But GTA Six's phone could be the most realistic one yet. Here are the apps that have been reported or rumored so far.

BuckMe. A banking app to manage your money.

A fitness app that tracks your sleep, activity, and tiredness. Meaning your character might actually get tired if you never rest.

Ryde Me. A ride-hailing app, basically Rockstar's take on Uber, for fast travel around the map.

Scooter Bros. An app that delivers your personal vehicle to you.

Snapmatic. A social photo feed, which returns from GTA Five.

Waink. An app that checks your car's value, and lets you control its locks, alarm, and tracker.

WhatUp. A messaging app.

And Peekhole. A rumored parody of online subscription creator sites. Classic Rockstar satire.

Now let's head out onto the water. Because Leonida is surrounded by it.

You could go jet skiing and pull off stunts on the waves. Take a boat out and cruise the coast, or head far offshore. Go surfing and catch a break at the beach.

You could try wakeboarding, paddle boarding on calmer water, or parasailing high above the coastline for an incredible view.

And underwater, you could go snorkeling in the shallows, or scuba diving to explore reefs, shipwrecks, and deeper water.

Finally, the big question. How big is the map?

According to early estimates, crossing the whole map by car could take around thirty to thirty-five minutes.

By plane, about seven minutes.

And if you decide to walk the whole thing? About one hundred and seventy-eight minutes. That's almost three hours on foot.

For comparison, GTA Five's map was already huge. And if these estimates are right, Leonida could be the biggest map Rockstar has ever made.
